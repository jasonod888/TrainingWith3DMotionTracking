# Section marker editor

The owner’s **Edit sections** page (`?page=review&id=…`) is a dedicated viewport-fitted workspace. It defaults to the focused instructor video when available, with a visible Video / 3D switch. Both views use the shared lesson frame clock, including the saved trim offsets. Switching views retains the playhead position and does not change markers.

## Editing

- The timeline is a rectangular clip track split into section blocks. The right-hand list contains **sections**, not markers: one boundary creates two section tiles, two boundaries create three, and so on. Every tile shows its start and end, including the final remainder. Selecting a tile highlights the corresponding clip block and updates the preview range.
- The **blue playback line** is visible from the beginning, so you can position the first split. It is confined to the selected section: dragging, keyboard steps, and transport steps all clamp to that section. Clicking a clip block selects its section without seeking within it; the matching right-hand tile updates too. Click or drag in the separate ruler strip above the selected block to seek. Clicks in that strip outside the selection do nothing. Select another section tile to change sections, including the final section after the last boundary. Clicking a boundary selects the section ending there. Selecting, moving, or removing a boundary also keeps the playback position within the resulting selection. Removing every marker restores a full-length preview range.
- Position the blue playback line, then choose **Add marker** or press **M**. You can refine the new numbered pin by dragging afterward. Each marker is inserted at the blue playback line’s current reconstructed frame. The blue line stays at the new boundary after insertion. This works in any selected section. Add marker is disabled at an existing boundary, at the beginning/end of the video, or where there is insufficient space for valid sections; move the line inside a section to enable it. Section names default to sequential numbers; naming is optional and belongs to the actual saved lesson section. The preview and inspector time update live during dragging; releasing the pin keeps that position.
- Select a section tile or numbered pin. Drag a boundary pin, use its arrow keys for one-frame changes (Shift for ten frames), or edit the selected section’s end time in seconds. The final end is fixed at the video duration. A typed time is applied on blur or Enter; Escape restores the displayed time.
- The start of the video is implicit and has no numbered pin or tile. A move without internal boundaries opens with a single full-length section tile and no numbered pins. Existing saved boundaries remain visible. Numbered markers cannot cross their neighbours. Every section remains contiguous, with full coverage of the trimmed move.
- Removing a section boundary joins the adjacent sections; the delete control removes the selected section’s ending boundary, or the final section’s starting boundary. Edited names are preserved; default section numbers follow the new order.
- Use the timeline zoom controls to separate closely spaced markers. Zoom follows the selected marker/playhead and never changes timings.
- **Play** previews the highlighted section from the blue line, stopping at its end boundary. At the section end, playing again restarts at the section's beginning. Pause/resume preserves the line's position. Space toggles playback outside text fields. The transport also provides individual frame steps and playback speed.
- **Save** stores the title and sections through the existing revision-aware sections endpoint. Leaving a dirty editor retains the existing unsaved-changes confirmation.

## Layout

The desktop layout gives the preview most of the screen, with a compact marker inspector beside it and the timeline below. The timeline sits directly beneath the preview and matches its width; the desktop inspector spans both rows. On phones the timeline stays directly beneath the preview, followed by a horizontal marker strip and compact optional name/time fields. Short landscape and portrait layouts reduce control spacing to preserve preview space. The page itself does not scroll; long marker lists scroll within their own region. Mobile save/error feedback remains visible above the transport.

## Verification

The editor keeps structural edits in one `commit` path, so adding, moving and removing boundaries update the player, selection and draft together. Frame placement validation is shared by the Add button and insertion operation. `SectionInspector` is memoized and receives stable edit callbacks, so playback snapshots do not rerender section tiles. `useSectionPlayback` requests editor animation frames only while playback is running, and publishes a final snapshot when it stops. Timeline pointer capture handles move/end events once at the track, and a resize observer measures the rail only when its width changes. Responsive styles are consolidated into shared rules and explicit viewport variants rather than repeated historical overrides.
